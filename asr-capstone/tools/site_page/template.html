Two ways to score the same transcript

Benchmark ruleLower-case everything, drop punctuation and filler words, then count the words that differ. This is how leaderboards rank engines.
Verbatim ruleCount every difference a court reporter would: capitals, punctuation, "uh", repeated words and the reporter's dash where someone is cut off.

Qwen3 hears the words best, but writes "court" for "Court", leaves out the dashes and drops repeats. Whisper hears slightly worse and writes more like a transcript. Every engine is two to four and a half times worse scored as a record.

The next datasets

CORAAL

Interviews from the Corpus of Regional African American Language: Rochester (2016) and Princeville (2004), 51 interviews and 27 hours, downloaded and queued for the three engines. The corpus Koenecke et al. used in 2020 to show commercial engines made twice as many errors for Black speakers. Notes in datasets/coraal.md.

Earnings-22

125 company earnings calls from 27 countries, 119 hours. Its reference is verbatim, with fillers, false starts and punctuation, so both scoring rules are fair on it; ten calls also come with a cleaned-up second reference. Not downloaded yet. Notes in datasets/earnings22.md.
