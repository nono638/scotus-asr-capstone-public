Judge bench

What this is, and what the labels mean

300 spots where a speech recognition engine's text differs from the reference transcript, from all four data sets. Play each one and say, by ear, what it is. A small model will learn from these labels which of the language model's answers pick out the cases worth showing; its answers are not on this page, so they can't sway you.

The order is random and mixed across data sets, so stopping after 150 or 200 still gives a fair spread.

1 Interesting
The engine says something different from what was said, in a way worth showing someone: a reversed meaning, a wrong fact or number, or a fluent change a reader wouldn't catch.
2 Real but dull
The engine is wrong, but it hardly matters, or anyone would see it: a garble, a slip, a small word.
3 Record is wrong
By ear the engine is right, or closer, and the reference transcript is the one that's mistaken.
4 Not an error
Both say the same thing: a spelling, a name, a form of the same word, or nothing wrong at all.
0 Can't tell
The audio is unclear, or the spot isn't in the clip.

Keys: 1–4 and 0 choose a label · Q the shorter clip, W the longer one · Space play again · ← → previous or next · N next unlabelled. Off while you type a note.

Jump to a case

Each case is a row of findings/judge/combo-v1.csv (75 per data set). The clip runs from 4 seconds before the spot to 4 seconds after, at least 10 seconds. The record line shows the reference's words around the spot, highlighted; the engine's words light up as they are spoken, and a yellow highlight marks a word that differs from the record. Earnings-22 has no audio here unless it was built with it: the calls have no stated licence.
